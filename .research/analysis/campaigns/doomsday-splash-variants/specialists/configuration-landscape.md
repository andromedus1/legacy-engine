# Current Doomsday configuration landscape

## Finding

The first post-Fantasticar slice does not show a pure BUG or Grixis branch. It shows three active
families: blue-black creature-transform, Esper Teferi/Swords, and a white-green hybrid that combines
Teferi/Swords with Veil/Carpet. The 12 published entries from August 10-18 comprise six blue-black,
four white-only, and two white-green lists; they represent 11 pilot names and 12 exact 75s.
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{1}`

That slice is evidence of what was published immediately after the ban, not a settled archetype
ranking. {inferred: temporal scope limits} Wizards made the Fantasticar ban effective August 10,
and the database has only nine calendar days of post-ban Doomsday entries through August 18.
`[ddv-landscape-wotc-aug-ban]{2}` `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{1}`

## Regime boundary

The Fantasticar is banned in Legacy effective August 10, 2026.
`[ddv-landscape-wotc-aug-ban]{1}` `[ddv-landscape-wotc-aug-ban]{2}` For this landscape, “post-ban”
therefore means August 10 onward. The older comparison is split into a clean pre-exposure window
(May 18-June 19) and the Fantasticar interval (June 20-August 9), matching the corpus extract rather
than treating all three periods as one stable format. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{5}`

## BUG: real lineage, absent from the post-ban slice

Pure BUG is a repeated configuration lineage rather than a theoretical splash. Between May 23 and
July 13, the database has 29 green-without-white-or-red source entries from 14 pilot names and 22
exact-list hashes. The clean pre-exposure portion contributes 19 rows / 8 pilots / 12 hashes; the
Fantasticar interval contributes 10 / 7 / 8. No pure BUG list occurs from August 10-18.
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{2}`

Its configurations are not all copies of one 75. The package ranges from Witherbloom Charm plus
Veil/Carpet to heavier four-Veil/two-Abrupt-Decay sideboards. Representative results include
nao_xx's second place on June 6, Dominic Rode's eighth place on July 6, and wakame's July 13 5-0.
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{2}` The public May 25 league page also displays a 5-0 nao_xx build with
Bayou, Tropical Island, two Witherbloom Charm, and the Doomsday/Flow State core.
`[ddv-landscape-mtgo-may25-bug]{1}` `[ddv-landscape-mtgo-may25-bug]{2}`

{inferred: temporal divergence} This establishes BUG as buildable and previously successful, but
not as currently adopted after the ban. Its last green-only corpus entry predates the ban by almost
a month, while green survives post-ban only in white-green lists. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{2}`

## Grixis Hexing Squelcher: developed package, concentrated evidence

The Hexing Squelcher Grixis package appears nine times from May 24 through June 27, but those rows
reduce to five pilot names and five exact decklists. nevilshute accounts for three entries and Zlatan87
for two, so nine published rows are not nine independent endorsements. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{3}`

The recurring configuration is concrete: Badlands plus Volcanic Island, one Squelcher main, two
side, and two Pyroblast; Zlatan87 instead uses two sideboard Squelcher and two Red Elemental Blast.
Results include four undefeated league publications, nevilshute's third place in a late-May Challenge, and
Wilson Prado's eighth place in a late-June paper event. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{3}` The primary
late-May MTGO page corroborates the Grixis decklist and its third-place finish, including one main and two
sideboard Squelcher, two Pyroblast, Badlands, Volcanic Island, and four Barrowgoyf.
`[ddv-landscape-mtgo-may31-grixis]{1}` `[ddv-landscape-mtgo-may31-grixis]{2}`
`[ddv-landscape-mtgo-may31-grixis]{3}`

No Squelcher Doomsday entry appears after late June. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{3}` {inferred:
temporal divergence} Grixis is therefore an evidenced 75-level option with a developed package,
but the refreshed corpus does not support describing it as a current post-ban branch.

## Adoption calibration

The landscape supports four different evidence statements, which should not be collapsed:

1. **Current published presence:** blue-black, Esper, white-permanent, and white-green are present in
   the August 10-18 slice. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{1}`
2. **Historical configuration viability:** pure BUG and Grixis Squelcher both have multiple pilots,
   exact lists, and successful published finishes. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{2}`
   `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{3}`
3. **Independent adoption:** weaker than row totals imply because pilots recur, exact lists recur,
   and older corpus rows include source/date duplication. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{4}`
4. **Comparative performance:** not established; league publication is selected, paper/Challenge
   samples are small, and the families occupy different time regimes. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{1}`
   `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{4}`

{inferred: evidence ordering} For immediate testing, this facet supplies current
configuration substrate for Dimir, Esper, and four-color; BUG and Grixis should enter the comparison
as historically evidenced challengers whose post-ban relevance must be re-tested, not assumed.

### Historical transformational branches

The Moonshadow package is a meaningful short-lived lineage: seven source rows, six pilots, and four
exact hashes in late June, with four Moonshadow sideboarded against a Fantasticar/Bauble main deck.
Its published results include Challenge wins and undefeated league entries.
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{7}` {inferred: legality qualification} The package itself is compatible
with a fixed maindeck because it was sideboard-only, but every observed base contains the now-banned
Fantasticar, so there is no attested legal-current implementation. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{10}`

The Cori-Steel Cutter transformation is also repeatable but more copied: six rows, four pilots, and
three exact hashes from mid-to-late July, all using the same red-dual/Fantasticar/Bauble structure
and four sideboard Cutter plus Barrowgoyf. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{7}` It is incompatible with a
fetch-only shared base because Badlands and Volcanic Island are main-deck requirements in all six
observations. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{10}`

Chancellor of the Annex is a pilot lineage rather than independent adoption: nine four-Chancellor
rows are attached mostly to wonderPreaux, with several source/date duplicates, plus one
ragavanejoyer row. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{7}` {inferred: structural compatibility} Its repeated
form is sideboard-only and therefore compatible with a fixed maindeck, but the corpus supplies no
post-ban continuation and weak independence.

### Core, tutor, and protection modules

The One Ring is a real older core branch but pilot-concentrated: 15 source entries, five pilots, and
eight hashes from late May through mid-July, mostly nao_xx green lists. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{6}`
It is not compatible with fetch-only interchange because Ring and the associated green cards and
duals occupy the main deck. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{6}` `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{10}`

Main-deck Cabal Ritual appears in 35 source entries through late July, while Personal Tutor spans
all three time partitions and remains in five post-ban lists. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{6}` These
are recurring speed/tutor modules rather than color families. {inferred: interchange test} Changing
between them alters non-fetch maindeck slots, so they cannot vary inside a strictly shared base.

The counter-protection axis also moves independently of color: main Flusterstorm is present in the
pre-ban windows but absent post-ban, while Misdirection reaches the post-ban slice in one main deck
and one sideboard. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{8}` {inferred: interchange test} Sideboard
Misdirection can preserve a fixed main; main-deck Flusterstorm or Misdirection cannot.

### Recurring flex modules versus noise

Sheoldred, Quantum Riddler, Phyrexian Arena, and Voice of Victory recur, but their distribution does
not support four new archetype names. Sheoldred is usually a sideboard singleton across unrelated
shells; Quantum Riddler spans 18 rows, 13 pilots, 15 hashes, and multiple chassis; Arena is six
sideboard rows across three pilots; Voice is attached to white and white-green families.
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{8}` {inferred: taxonomy} Treat these as flex/value modules. A sideboard
singleton is structurally compatible with a fixed main, except where its color requires a main-deck
source; main-deck Riddler or Voice is not.

Vision Charm and Cling to Dust are repeated utility choices but pilot-concentrated rather than
independent lineages: clan supplies most Vision Charm entries, and only two pilot names account for
Cling. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{8}` Dark Confidant/Skeletal Scrying, Reanimate, and the
Thundertrap Trainer/Temporal Mastery Yorion build remain experiments: the Yorion configuration is
one underlying 80-card list amplified across three source URLs, and the Reanimate records include
duplicate-source pairs. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{9}` These observations are insufficient to
promote them into candidate families.

The Fantasticar/Bauble chassis was the large pre-ban branch—173 of 226 interval rows contain
Fantasticar and 125 contain Bauble—but it is now legally unavailable. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{6}`
`[ddv-landscape-wotc-aug-ban]{2}` It remains useful only as historical context for why Moonshadow,
Cutter, and some sideboard-contained Esper pivots lack a legal-current base.

## Disconfirming analysis

The post-ban query was searched specifically for green-only and red/Hexing lists. It returned none,
which disconfirms any claim that BUG or Grixis is already represented in the 12-entry post-ban
slice. It did return two green-white lists, preventing the broader but false statement that green
has disappeared from Doomsday. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{1}`

The older corpus was searched for unique pilots and exact-list hashes rather than card occurrence
alone. That check disconfirms treating nine Squelcher rows as nine independent builds: they are five
pilots and five exact 75s, with repeat appearances by nevilshute and Zlatan87.
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{3}` It also disconfirms treating BUG as one copied list: 29 source rows
span 22 hashes, although duplicate/date defects make the raw row count unsuitable as a tournament
appearance count. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{2}` `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{4}`

The public MTGO pages were checked against the database's representative Grixis, Esper, and hybrid
rows. Their card packages and final ranks agree with the database extracts.
`[ddv-landscape-mtgo-may31-grixis]{1}` `[ddv-landscape-mtgo-may31-grixis]{2}`
`[ddv-landscape-mtgo-aug15]{1}` `[ddv-landscape-mtgo-aug15]{2}`
`[ddv-landscape-mtgo-aug15]{3}` This corroboration does not cure dependence elsewhere in the
corpus or supply missing failed-league runs.

The wide-net scan searched every main- and sideboard card after May 18, then checked source rows,
pilot names, exact hashes, dates, and co-occurring cards. This disconfirms promoting every recurring
singleton into a variant: Quantum Riddler and Sheoldred recur across unrelated chassis, Chancellor
and Cling are pilot-concentrated, and Yorion/Thundertrap is duplicate-amplified.
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{7}` `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{8}`
`[ddv-landscape-current-db]{9}` Conversely, the same test prevents dismissing Moonshadow and Cutter
as one-offs: both have multiple pilots and exact configurations, although both are tied to the
Fantasticar period. `[ddv-landscape-current-db]{7}`
