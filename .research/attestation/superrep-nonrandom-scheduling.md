# Avoiding Bias Due to Nonrandom Scheduling When Modeling Trends in Home-Field Advantage

## Summary

Karl studies sports results whose schedule is not randomly assigned. Across six sports and 18
seasons, the paper compares fixed- and random-team-effect estimates and uses simulation to show that
the schedule structure can bias a mixed-model estimate. Modeling team ability reduces but does not
eliminate the bias when stronger teams systematically receive different assignments. Restricting the
analysis to a more comparable schedule changes the inference space rather than automatically
justifying generalization beyond it.

## Key passages

- Abstract and §2: the paper compares assumptions made by fixed- and random-effect models about the
  stochastic schedule, then evaluates bias with simulated data generated from observed schedules.
- §3.2.2, lines 134–136 in the fetched HTML: stronger college teams often schedule more home games;
  conditioning on team abilities “ameliorates, but does not eliminate” bias from those nonrandom
  assignments.
- §3.2.2, lines 124–131: the bias assessment holds each realized schedule fixed while resampling
  fitted team effects and residuals.
- §3.2.2, line 173: restricting to intraconference games limits the inference space; additional
  assumptions or model structure are needed to generalize to interconference games.
