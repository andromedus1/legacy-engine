# Metrics of Calibration for Probabilistic Predictions

## Summary

Arrieta-Ibarra and colleagues examine diagnostics for discrepancies between predicted probabilities
and observed event rates. They show that conventional reliability diagrams and scalar calibration
metrics depend on bin or kernel width, creating a trade-off between noise reduction and local
resolution. They propose cumulative differences between observed and expected outcomes as a
bin-free diagnostic whose slopes expose local miscalibration and whose deviations from zero yield
scalar summaries.

## Key passages

1. Reliability diagrams are intended to detect statistically significant discrepancies between
   probabilistic predictions and outcomes (abstract).
2. Histogram and kernel calibration displays necessarily trade statistical confidence against
   resolution through their width choices (abstract).
3. Cumulative-difference plots avoid explicit bin selection; calibration corresponds to a nearly
   horizontal cumulative-difference trace near zero (abstract).
