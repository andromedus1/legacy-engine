## Summary

Christopher Walton's "Building Sideboards in Legacy" (CoolStuffInc) argues that strong Legacy
sideboards favor broad, overlapping answers over narrow single-target hate, and warns against
over-dedicating slots. Cards that attack a whole strategy class (e.g. graveyard hate that hits
"nearly all graveyard strategies") earn their slots because they retain value across many
opponents, whereas cards effective against only one deck waste space unless that deck is a large,
known share of the field. The piece also frames the failure mode of over-sideboarding — committing
so many slots to feared matchups that the deck loses the threats it needs — and notes that if a
matchup is so bad it forces most of your board, the better fix is often to change decks rather than
to spend the whole sideboard answering it.

## Key passages

- "If you oversideboard, you run the risk of having too many answers and not enough threats, which
  is often worse than just not having a board."
- On broad overlapping hate (Tormod's Crypt / Relic of Progenitus): "both are staples and attack
  nearly all graveyard strategies, without being weak to any one in specific."
- On narrow single-target hate: a card like that "would generally be a waste of space ... as it's
  only truly effective against one deck."
