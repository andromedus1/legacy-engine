# Higgins and Thompson — Quantifying heterogeneity in a meta-analysis

## Summary

Higgins and Thompson derive measures for how between-study heterogeneity affects a pooled
analysis. Their work distinguishes a pooled estimate's sampling uncertainty from variability
among source-specific effects and proposes `H`, `R`, and `I²` as heterogeneity summaries. The
measures describe the impact and extent of between-study heterogeneity.

## Key passages

1. The abstract states that the extent of heterogeneity partly determines how difficult it is to
   draw an overall conclusion.
2. The paper derives `H`, `R`, and `I²`; `R` compares the standard error under random-effects and
   fixed-effect summaries, while `I²` expresses the proportion of total variation attributable to
   heterogeneity.
3. The measures were designed to be less dependent on study count and effect metric than the
   conventional heterogeneity test.
4. The authors recommend reporting `H` or `I²` in preference to relying only on a test for the
   existence of heterogeneity.
