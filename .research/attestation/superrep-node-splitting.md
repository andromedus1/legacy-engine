# Automated generation of node-splitting models for assessment of inconsistency in network meta-analysis

## Summary

van Valkenhoef and colleagues formalize node splitting for a Bayesian comparison network. For a
comparison with both kinds of support, the method estimates one parameter from direct evidence and
another from the remaining indirect network, then assesses agreement. The consistency equations are
not consequences of graph connectivity alone: they rely on an exchangeability assumption. A shared
heterogeneity parameter can stabilize the split even when direct evidence is small, but the split is
only available where both direct and indirect paths exist.

## Key passages

- §2.1: exchangeability renders indirect comparisons consistent with direct comparisons through the
  network's consistency equations.
- §2.2: node splitting separates a comparison into direct and indirect parameters and assesses
  whether the two agree.
- §2.2: the indirect parameter is estimated from the network after removing the direct evidence for
  that comparison.
- §2.2: sharing the heterogeneity parameter permits estimation when direct evidence contains few
  trials.
