# Assessing the Generalizability of Randomized Trial Results to Target Populations

## Summary

Stuart, Bradshaw, and Leaf distinguish accuracy for the observed study participants from relevance
to a target population chosen for a decision. They attribute failures of external validity to an
unspecified target, unrepresentative enrollment, or a later decision concerning a different target.
They also distinguish enrolling a typical or average unit from enrolling heterogeneous units that
span the target population. A typical representative therefore cannot substitute for estimating an
average across heterogeneous target members.
