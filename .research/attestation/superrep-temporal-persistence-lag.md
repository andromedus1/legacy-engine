# Disentangling group and link persistence in dynamic stochastic block models

## Summary

Barucca, Lillo, Mazzarisi, and Tantari analyze a dynamic stochastic block model in which both group
memberships and observed links persist. Their central counterexample to naive temporal smoothing is
time-lagged inference: persistent observations can cause a snapshot procedure to identify a past
community assignment more strongly than the present assignment. They derive when a positive lag
appears and propose a correction in their model.

The exact stochastic-block assumptions do not transfer directly to matchup data. The load-bearing
result is narrower: persistence in observations and persistence in latent identity are distinct, and
pooling them indiscriminately can make an apparently stable classification stale.
