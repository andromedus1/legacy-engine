# Quantifying heterogeneity in a meta-analysis

## Summary

Higgins and Thompson derive H, R, and I-squared as measures of heterogeneity that are less directly
dependent on the number of studies than the conventional heterogeneity test. I-squared expresses the
proportion of total variation in effect estimates attributable to between-study heterogeneity. The
paper also shows substantial uncertainty in these measures when evidence is limited; they quantify a
pattern but do not prove homogeneity when their estimate is small or imprecise.

## Key passages

- Abstract: the conventional test for heterogeneity depends on the number of studies.
- Abstract: I-squared is a transformation of H describing the proportion of total variation due to
  heterogeneity.
- Results: the proposed measures were evaluated by mathematical criteria and simulation and applied
  to published meta-analyses.
- The article's uncertainty treatment derives interval estimates rather than treating the point
  statistic as known.
