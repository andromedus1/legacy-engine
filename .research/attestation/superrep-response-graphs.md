# Navigating the Landscape of Multiplayer Games

## Summary

Omidshafiei and collaborators represent a game as a directed response graph whose nodes are
strategies and whose edges encode likely improvement moves. Their spectral construction compares
nodes by their relationships to every other node, so redundant strategies occupy similar relational
roles even when no scalar strength makes them equivalent. After clustering, the method contracts
nodes and sums edge probabilities while retaining directed relationships between the resulting
groups. Redundant rock–paper–scissors is an explicit motivating case.

## Key passages

> “strategies have similar relational roles with respect to all other strategies in the game.” — Methods, spectral response graphs

> “we contract clustered nodes (summing edge probabilities accordingly)” — Methods, contracted response graphs
