## Summary

Cochrane Handbook chapter 10 (Analysing data and undertaking meta-analyses), §10.10 Heterogeneity —
the clinical-trials field's formalization of "may I pool these units at all?", which is the same
question a superarchetype cell asks about its member archetypes. Two instruments: a Chi-squared
(Cochran's Q) test of whether observed differences between units are compatible with chance alone,
and the I² statistic, which reports the percentage of variability in effect estimates attributable to
real heterogeneity rather than sampling error. The handbook supplies rough interpretation bands for
I² (0–40% might not be important; 30–60% moderate; 50–90% substantial; 75–100% considerable) and two
load-bearing caveats: the Chi² test has low power precisely when studies are few or small (so a
non-significant test is NOT evidence of homogeneity), and the I² thresholds themselves can mislead
because what matters is the magnitude and direction of the effects, not the statistic alone.

## Key passages

- The test: "A Chi2 (χ2, or chi-squared) test is included in the forest plots in Cochrane reviews. It
  assesses whether observed differences in results are compatible with chance alone."
- Low power (the one-sided-evidence caveat): "Care must be taken in the interpretation of the Chi2
  test, since it has low power in the (common) situation of a meta-analysis when studies have small
  sample size or are few in number."
- I² definition: "A useful statistic for quantifying inconsistency is the I2 statistic...This
  describes the percentage of the variability in effect estimates that is due to heterogeneity rather
  than sampling error (chance)."
- Interpretation bands: "0% to 40%: might not be important; 30% to 60%: may represent moderate
  heterogeneity; 50% to 90%: may represent substantial heterogeneity; 75% to 100%: considerable
  heterogeneity."
- Thresholds mislead: "Thresholds for the interpretation of the I2 statistic can be misleading, since
  the importance of inconsistency depends on several factors" — including the magnitude and direction
  of effects and the strength of evidence for the inconsistency.
