# Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery — Probabilistic Forecasts, Calibration and Sharpness

## Summary

The paper defines calibration as statistical consistency between probabilistic predictions and
realized observations, distinguishes it from sharpness, and develops diagnostic plots and proper
scores for evaluating probabilistic forecasts.

## Key passages

1. The abstract defines calibration as consistency between forecasts and observations and
   sharpness as concentration of the predictive distributions; it proposes maximizing sharpness
   subject to calibration (lines 79–80).
2. Scoring rules summarize predictive performance by addressing calibration and sharpness
   together (lines 535–536).
3. A proper scoring rule has expected penalty minimized by the data-generating distribution; a
   strictly proper score has a unique minimum (lines 535–537).
4. For binary events, the paper identifies the Brier score as a proper score and notes its
   calibration/refinement decomposition (lines 548–556).
