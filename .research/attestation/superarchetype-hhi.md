## Summary

The Herfindahl–Hirschman index — the concentration measure that answers "is this aggregate really one
member's record?". HHI is the sum of squared shares; in fractional form it ranges from 1/N (perfectly
even across N participants) to 1.0 (one participant holds everything). Its reciprocal 1/H is the
"equivalent (or effective) number" of participants, which is the interpretable form for a gate: a
cluster cell whose member-contribution HHI is 0.5 has an effective member count of exactly 2,
regardless of how many members nominally belong to it. The antitrust thresholds (unconcentrated below
0.15, moderately concentrated 0.15–0.25, highly concentrated above 0.25) are recorded here for
completeness but are calibrated to markets with many firms — an evenly-split FOUR-member group already
sits exactly at the "highly concentrated" line — so the effective-number reading transfers to small
groups while the thresholds do not.

## Key passages

- Definition: "HHI is calculated by squaring the market share of each competing firm in the industry
  and then summing the resulting numbers." (HHI = Σ(MS_i)²)
- Range: the index ranges from 1/N to 1.0 in fractional form (0 to 10,000 in basis points).
- Effective number: "in the more general case of unequal market share, 1/H is called 'equivalent (or
  effective) number of firms.'"
- U.S. DOJ concentration bands cited in the article: below 0.01 highly competitive; below 0.15
  unconcentrated; 0.15 to 0.25 moderately concentrated, which "will raise anti-competitive concerns";
  above 0.25 highly concentrated, which "will be considered anti competitive".
