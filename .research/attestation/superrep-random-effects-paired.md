# Random Effects in Paired-Comparison Experiments Using the Bradley-Terry Model

## Summary

Lancaster and Quade generalize Bradley–Terry paired comparisons by treating preference parameters as
random across a sampled population of judges. The construction induces correlation among outcomes
involving the same judge. It is intended to separate judge-level variation from treatment variation
and to account for nonindependence created by repeated judgments.

## Key passages

- Abstract: the ordinary model assumes treatment-rating parameters are the same for every
  comparison.
- Abstract: random preference parameters induce correlation among outcomes involving the same judge.
- Abstract: the model can isolate variation due to judges from variation in treatments and account
  for nonindependence of judgments.
