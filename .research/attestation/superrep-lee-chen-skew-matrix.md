# Pairwise Comparisons without Stochastic Transitivity: Model, Theory and Applications

## Summary

Lee and Chen model pairwise outcome probabilities through an approximately low-dimensional
skew-symmetric matrix rather than a scalar merit ordering. The skew-symmetric construction permits
cycles while retaining direction reversal. They develop likelihood-based estimators for settings
where only a small proportion of pairs are observed and state minimax-rate guarantees that adapt to
the observation sparsity. Simulations and real-data examples compare the method with
Bradley–Terry. The fetched version is a recent arXiv preprint and should be treated as emerging,
not settled, evidence.

## Key passages

- Abstract: Bradley–Terry and Thurstone impose a global ranking and stochastic-transitivity
  constraints that may fail in games involving multiple skills or strategies.
- Abstract: pairwise probabilities are determined by an approximately low-dimensional
  skew-symmetric matrix.
- Abstract: the likelihood-based algorithms allow only a small proportion of pairs to be observed;
  the paper claims minimax-rate optimality adapting to sparsity.
- Abstract: simulations and real-data analysis support improved performance relative to
  Bradley–Terry.
