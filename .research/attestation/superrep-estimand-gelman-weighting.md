# Struggles with Survey Weighting and Regression Modeling

## Summary

Gelman formulates poststratification as estimation of a finite-population quantity from cell-level
estimates and known population cell counts. The population mean is the population-count-weighted
sum of the cell means. The treatment also makes the sampling condition explicit: selection must be
ignorable within poststratification cells, or relative selection probabilities within cells must be
known. When full joint population cells are themselves sparse or unknown, margins or modeled cell
counts replace direct poststratification and introduce another estimation problem.

## Key passages

> “The purpose of poststratification is to correct for known differences between sample and population.” — p.3, Poststratification

> “the data are collected by simple random sample within each of the J poststrata.” — p.3, Poststratification

> “The population mean of any survey response can be written as a sum over poststrata” — p.3, equation 1
