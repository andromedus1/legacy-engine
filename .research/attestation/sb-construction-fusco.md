## Summary

Roman Fusco's "Maximizing Your MTG Sideboard" (CoolStuffInc) distinguishes dedicated silver-bullet
hate from flexible/modal answers and gives concrete copy-count guidance: dedicated swap packages
typically run three to four copies (so you reliably draw and swap them as a block), while flexible
slots lean on one-ofs that cover several matchups. Flexible cards — answers that are live in more
than one matchup — stretch the effective size of a 15-card board. The article's central discipline
is that every slot must earn its place ("if you don't know when you're bringing it in, it shouldn't
be there") and that a sideboard cannot try to beat everything; it must pick a lane. This grounds
both the commit-vs-insurance framing (dedicated 3-4-ofs as commitments, flexible one-ofs as
insurance) and the diminishing value of redundant narrow copies.

## Key passages

- "Silver bullets, or 'hate' cards, are the type of card you bring in to stop a specific part of
  your opponent's gameplan."
- "your sideboard usually has three or four copies of specific cards to make dedicated swaps."
- "my sideboard has more one-ofs for more specific matchups"
- "cards like Devout Decree are both good against Mono-Red Strategies and Dimir strategies, making
  it a flexible removal spell."
- "Make every card earn its slot. If you don't know when you're bringing it in, it shouldn't be
  there."
- "You can't try to beat everything; you have to pick a lane and stay in it if you want to have
  success."
