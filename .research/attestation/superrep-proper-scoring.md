# Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation

## Summary

Gneiting and Raftery formalize scoring rules for probabilistic forecasts. A proper rule makes the
forecaster optimize expected score by reporting its actual predictive distribution; strict
propriety makes that optimum unique. The paper treats logarithmic and quadratic/Brier scores as
examples and frames forecast quality as sharpness subject to calibration. This supplies an
outcome-based basis for comparing probability models rather than selecting them by coverage or
apparent in-sample fit.

## Key passages

> “proper scoring rules encourage the forecaster to make careful assessments and to be honest.” — p.359, Abstract

> “the goal of probabilistic forecasting is to maximize the sharpness ... subject to calibration.” — p.359, Introduction
