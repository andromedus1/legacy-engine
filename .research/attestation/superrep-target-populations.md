# Estimating predicted probabilities from logistic regression: different methods correspond to different target populations

## Summary

Muller and MacLehose compare marginal standardization with prediction at representative covariate
values after logistic regression. Their central distinction is interpretive: each averaging rule
answers for a different population. Marginal standardization averages individual predictions using
the target population's observed covariate distribution, whereas prediction at means or modes
describes a constructed stratum and can describe no actual observations when binary covariates are
set to their means. The paper therefore makes target-population weights part of the estimand rather
than a post-model implementation detail.

## Key passages

> “That each method corresponds to a different target population is underappreciated in practice.” — Abstract, Background

> “Marginal standardization allows inference to the total population from which data are drawn.” — Abstract, Results

> “prediction at the means corresponds to a stratum that does not include any real-life observations.” — Abstract, Results
