What is Agency %? — the metric and each column
Agency % = min( adjusted field win rate , worst grounded matchup ) × 100 — how much of the match result your own play keeps in the worst case. The adjusted field WR is your shrunk win rate weighted by the current field's archetype shares (are you competitive in general?). The worst grounded matchup is your floor against any opponent you can actually be paired into (are you competitive against anyone?). A high-agency deck never hands the result to the pairing screen: it concedes no free wins to the room. Theory under test: maximum agency = most fun.
  • Measured cells only — a matchup counts when its sample is n ≥ 8 matches. Era-windowed cells are preferred; when the era window is too thin, a fallback cell is used — and the fallback pools matches only since the last ban that affected either deck in the pair (ran a banned card in ≥25% of its pre-ban lists), labeled BA date. A true full-corpus cell (FC) exists only when neither deck was ever ban-affected. The Nadu rule: a banned engine's matches never inflate a row — in either direction.
  • Family fallback in the expanded ledger — fewer blank cells and honest leans, never grounded coverage. When a matchup is unmeasured in both windows, the ledger may fill it from the deck's superarchetype (strategy family), walking a fixed ladder: imputed — the deck's family siblings' record against that exact opponent, shown only under an earned family-coherence license, CI widened by the family's profile dispersion; pooled vs family — the deck's own record against every member of the opponent's family (an intra-family chip flags how much of that pool is the deck's own family); else a family range — the member split with the named refusal (dominated by <member>, heterogeneous pool, local veto, comparability desert) and no point estimate. Borrowed evidence clears a stricter gate than a deck's own cells (pooled n_eff ≥ 30; imputed pool n ≥ 25 + license + per-cell veto, vs n ≥ 8 for measured cells). Leans are ledger-only: they never enter agency, adj field WR, floor, coverage, or the strata. The I² heterogeneity gate is one-sided evidence — a high I² is a reliable stop, but a low I² is never a certificate of exchangeability, so passing the gate never promotes a pooled estimate to measured status. Window mix, current-regime share, license, and member records ride each chip's tooltip; a pool with < 50% current-regime evidence carries an amber ◦mostly pre-regime marker; the registry's own provenance (and its stale-taxonomy warning, when it fires) is in the audit header above.
  • A thin cell must prove its hole — the row's floor can only be set by a cell with n ≥ 20, or by a thinner measured cell whose 95% CI upper bound is still below 50% (an 0-8 qualifies; a 2-6 is ambiguity, not evidence). This keeps the min() from being won by noise: without the gate, the deck with the most measured cells tends to show the lowest floor purely mechanically.
  • Coverage counts against the floor — unmeasured field share is shown per row; a floor claim over 70% of the field is a 70% claim.
  • Blowouts — count of measured current-field matchups where you're getting run over: shrunk WR < 40% is a full blowout, 40–45% is a half. The chip shows full✕ + half½ of N measured. Floor is your single worst hole; blowout count is how many holes — a richer read on "competitive against anyone." Classified on the shrunk estimate so a thin 3-5 run doesn't read as a blowout; unmeasured cells are excluded entirely (a shrinkage prior is not a blowout).
  • % meta that blows you out — the blowout count weighted by field share: the full share of every sub-40% opponent plus half the share of every 40–45% opponent. This is how much of the room you sit down against at a structural disadvantage — a small count of blowouts against big-share decks matters more than many against fringe brews. Measured share only, so it's a lower bound (unmeasured opponents can't be counted).
  • Grounded rows — the top-8 field opponents must each be measured and ≥ 80% of field share-mass covered. Rows failing this are listed separately in gray: their agency number is a lean, not a claim.
How the numbers are made — matches, shrinkage, era windows, camps
  • Where matches come from — decklists are mirrored from the public tournament cache (MTGO + Melee + CardsRealm) and labeled with archetypes by a rules engine. Matchup win rates come only from rounds-bearing events (Challenges and paper events publish pairings; MTGO leagues publish decklists but no rounds). So a deck's meta share and its matchup sample are different-sized populations — a big field share with thin matchup cells is normal, not a bug. A match counts when one player took strictly more games ("2-1", "2-0"); draws and byes are excluded from win rates.
  • What "current field" means — the field is every published decklist since the latest confirmed B&R change (the audit header names the ban): a ban invalidates the room it changed, so share estimates never mix regimes. % of meta is a deck's share of all window decklists — raw counts, one published list = one vote, no win-rate weighting. Know what gets published, because the field inherits that bias: MTGO leagues publish only 5-0 lists, challenges publish top finishers, paper events publish whatever the venue uploads — and online + paper are blended here (the engine tracks venue splits, but this page doesn't). Decklists the labeling rules can't classify surface as Unknown rather than being dropped. Note the two "currents": the field window is the ban regime; the ◦not current marker uses a shorter horizon (the last 4 corpus weeks), because "legal in this regime" and "actually being played this month" are different questions.
  • Why two win rates per matchup — raw is literally wins/n: what happened, but noisy at small n (a 3-1 cell reads 75% and means almost nothing). The headline number is a Beta-Binomial shrunk estimate: it behaves as if the cell started with ~15 phantom matches at a prior win rate, so small samples get pulled toward the prior and big samples converge to raw. The gap between the two numbers is itself a signal — a big gap means "small sample, don't trust raw".
  • The prior is hierarchical, not flat — a matchup cell shrinks toward the deck's own overall win rate (itself first shrunk toward 50%), so an unknown matchup for a strong deck reads as "probably decent", not "coin flip". A camp's cell shrinks toward its parent archetype's same matchup with that camp's decks removed — camps inherit the parent's profile until their own data speaks. When an era boundary truncates a cell below n=100, the pre-boundary value serves as its prior (labeled in the cell's provenance), so history informs but never impersonates the current era.
  • Era windows ("since" column) — every deck's history is bucketed by week and scanned by four change detectors: card-presence cliffs/ramps (a ban or a new-set adoption is a visible step), composition shifts in the flex slots, share-of-field breaks, and win-rate corroboration (never primary evidence on its own). Nearby candidates merge; the whole fleet of boundaries then passes one Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery screen (α=0.05) so ~150 entities screened every refresh don't accumulate false eras; a surviving boundary is still rejected if too few decks exist after it. The last accepted boundary is the deck's stable_since; each boundary is attributed to a ban/release when the evidence names one (e.g. "release: Flow State adoption"), and an unattributed disturbance raises a drift alarm instead of silently re-windowing.
  • How a cell picks its window — a matchup A-vs-B pools matches back to the later of the two decks' stable_since dates (both sides must be in their current form). If that era window has n<8, a fallback cell is shown instead — pooled since the later of the two decks' ban-affectedness dates (labeled BA date), or full history (FC) only when neither deck was ever materially hit by a ban. More data, may blend older builds of the same legal engine — but never a banned one: the Nadu-era Cephalid and Candelabra-era Forge matches are structurally excluded from every fallback that touches those decks.
  • Sample tiers — tier words on this page (e.g. the field-basis tier in the audit header) are fixed thresholds on n: speculative <30, evolving 30–99, established ≥100.
  • Camps (subarchetypes) — found by clustering each archetype's mainboard flex slots (TF-IDF → SVD → HDBSCAN), then gated: the split must survive bootstrap re-clustering (co-membership stability ≥0.9 over 50 resamples) and every camp needs a real sample plus clearly divergent signature cards. Camps are a staged overlay — applied for analysis, never silently promoted to taxonomy — and decks that fit no camp stay honestly [unlabeled]. Camp field share = parent share × the camp's fraction of the parent's current-window decks. All camp matchup cells come from ONE multi-split matrix built over every staged parent at once — numerically identical to the old one-matrix-per-parent builds (parity-tested in the engine), but every camp now coexists in a single matrix.
  • Cross-camp P(best) — the camp table's P(best) column comes from one shared-field Monte-Carlo: sample the field once per draw (Dirichlet over the parent-level window counts, fixed seed), score every candidate — all camps plus all unsplit archetypes — against that same sampled field, and count how often each is the argmax. Sharing the field draw is what makes the number comparable across camps of different parents. Cells are the same era-preferred / ban-scoped ones the ledger shows; candidates below 5% measured coverage are excluded (their score would be imputation noise) and show n/a instead.
  • No significance theater — the clustering gate is stability-based on purpose: running a t-test on data the clustering already saw would double-dip and inflate false positives. Where a real multiple-testing control exists (era boundaries), it's BH-FDR; where it can't exist honestly, the page shows sample sizes and tiers instead.

Agency map — general strength vs floor

One dot per archetype with a computable adjusted WR and floor. Blue = grounded floor: the deck's top-8 field opponents are each measured (n ≥ 8) and ≥ 80% of field share-mass is covered, so the floor is a measured claim. Gray = thin floor (a lean): that grounding failed — unknown matchups sit near the deck's own shrunk average, and agency = min over cells can only fall as more get measured — so the dot's floor is an upper bound, not a claim. Filled dot = current; hollow dot = not current (fewer than 5 decks in the last 4 corpus weeks). Labels mark the top 5 grounded-and-current decks by agency; hover any dot for its numbers.

Strategy families → archetypes → camps exploratory

Navigation, not a recommendation. Strategy families are outcome-blind composition groups. They help explain the room and pool sparse matchup evidence, but the archetype table below remains the decision-authoritative Best Call surface until the future-only predictive/decision benchmark passes. A low I² is never proof that members are exchangeable.

Archetypes

Click a row to expand its full matchup ledger — one line per current-field opponent: the chip after the name is that opponent's % of meta; the first number is the shrunk estimate, raw is wins/n; the window chip is era (era-windowed), BA date (fallback since the later ban that affected either deck), or FC (full corpus — neither deck ever ban-affected); "unmeasured" = n<8, excluded from floor and blowouts.
Column notes: decks/4wk = the deck's corpus count over the last 4 corpus weeks; ◦not current marks names with fewer than 5 decks in that span (historical form, not a live pick); window = the row's era window (since date = stable_since from era detection; full history = no accepted era boundary); % of meta = share of all decks in the field window (audit header); grounded row = the top-8 field opponents are each measured (n ≥ 8) and ≥ 80% of field share-mass is covered — its floor is a measured claim; ≤ before an agency value = ungrounded (thin-floor) row, the value is an upper bound.

Subarchetypes (camps)

Same columns, ledger, and markers as the archetype table above, plus P(best): the probability this deck is the best call for the current field — one shared Monte-Carlo where every camp and every unsplit archetype is scored against the same sampled field (parent-level Dirichlet, fixed seed; parameters in the audit header), on the same era-preferred / ban-scoped cells the ledger shows. Because all candidates share one field draw, the values are comparable across camps of different parents — the single number the old per-parent matrices could never provide. Candidates need ≥ 5% measured field coverage; rows below that show n/a with their coverage (their score would be pure imputation). The chip is the risk-adjusted lower-quartile field WR (Q25); S* marks coverage < 85% — a full-field estimate leaning on imputation for the unmeasured share, including each camp's own parent (that cell is absent by construction).
Camp % of meta = parent share × camp fraction among the parent's field-window decks. [unlabeled] = the parent's decks that fit no discovered camp (cluster noise / new builds the frozen membership hasn't seen) — surfaced honestly, never absorbed into a camp. Camp labels are staged-candidate provenance (speculative overlay, not promoted taxonomy).
