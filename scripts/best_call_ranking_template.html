Best Deck / Best Call — ranked by Agency

What is Agency %? — the metric and each column
Agency % = min( adjusted field win rate , worst grounded matchup ) × 100 — how much of the match result your own play keeps in the worst case. The adjusted field WR is your shrunk win rate weighted by the current field's archetype shares (are you competitive in general?). The worst grounded matchup is your floor against any opponent you can actually be paired into (are you competitive against anyone?). A high-agency deck never hands the result to the pairing screen: it concedes no free wins to the room. Theory under test: maximum agency = most fun.
  • Measured cells only — a matchup counts when its sample is n ≥ 8 matches. Era-windowed cells are preferred; when the era window is too thin, the full-corpus cell is used and labeled FC (never silently dropped, never a shrinkage prior masquerading as a floor).
  • Coverage counts against the floor — unmeasured field share is shown per row; a floor claim over 70% of the field is a 70% claim.
  • Blowouts — count of measured current-field matchups where you're getting run over: raw WR < 40% is a full blowout, 40–45% is a half. The chip shows full✕ + half½ of N measured. Floor is your single worst hole; blowout count is how many holes — a richer read on "competitive against anyone." Thin cells are excluded (a shrinkage prior is not a blowout).
  • % meta that blows you out — the blowout count weighted by field share: the full share of every sub-40% opponent plus half the share of every 40–45% opponent. This is how much of the room you sit down against at a structural disadvantage — a small count of blowouts against big-share decks matters more than many against fringe brews. Measured share only, so it's a lower bound (unmeasured opponents can't be counted).
  • Grounded rows — the top-8 field opponents must each be measured and ≥ 80% of field share-mass covered. Rows failing this are listed separately in gray: their agency number is a lean, not a claim.
How the numbers are made — matches, shrinkage, era windows, camps
  • Where matches come from — decklists are mirrored from the public tournament cache (MTGO + Melee + CardsRealm) and labeled with archetypes by a rules engine. Matchup win rates come only from rounds-bearing events (Challenges and paper events publish pairings; MTGO leagues publish decklists but no rounds). So a deck's meta share and its matchup sample are different-sized populations — a big field share with thin matchup cells is normal, not a bug. A match counts when one player took strictly more games ("2-1", "2-0"); draws and byes are excluded from win rates.
  • Why two win rates per matchup — raw is literally wins/n: what happened, but noisy at small n (a 3-1 cell reads 75% and means almost nothing). The headline number is a Beta-Binomial shrunk estimate: it behaves as if the cell started with ~15 phantom matches at a prior win rate, so small samples get pulled toward the prior and big samples converge to raw. The gap between the two numbers is itself a signal — a big gap means "small sample, don't trust raw".
  • The prior is hierarchical, not flat — a matchup cell shrinks toward the deck's own overall win rate (itself first shrunk toward 50%), so an unknown matchup for a strong deck reads as "probably decent", not "coin flip". A camp's cell shrinks toward its parent archetype's same matchup with that camp's decks removed — camps inherit the parent's profile until their own data speaks. When an era boundary truncates a cell below n=100, the pre-boundary value serves as its prior (labeled in the cell's provenance), so history informs but never impersonates the current era.
  • Era windows ("since" column) — every deck's history is bucketed by week and scanned by four change detectors: card-presence cliffs/ramps (a ban or a new-set adoption is a visible step), composition shifts in the flex slots, share-of-field breaks, and win-rate corroboration (never primary evidence on its own). Nearby candidates merge; the whole fleet of boundaries then passes one Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery screen (α=0.05) so ~150 entities screened every refresh don't accumulate false eras; a surviving boundary is still rejected if too few decks exist after it. The last accepted boundary is the deck's stable_since; each boundary is attributed to a ban/release when the evidence names one (e.g. "release: Flow State adoption"), and an unattributed disturbance raises a drift alarm instead of silently re-windowing.
  • How a cell picks its window — a matchup A-vs-B pools matches back to the later of the two decks' stable_since dates (both sides must be in their current form). If that era window has n<8, the full-history cell is shown instead and labeled FC — more data, but it may blend old builds; the label keeps that trade-off visible.
  • Sample tiers — tier words on this page (e.g. the field-basis tier in the audit header) are fixed thresholds on n: speculative <30, evolving 30–99, established ≥100.
  • Camps (subarchetypes) — found by clustering each archetype's mainboard flex slots (TF-IDF → SVD → HDBSCAN), then gated: the split must survive bootstrap re-clustering (co-membership stability ≥0.9 over 50 resamples) and every camp needs a real sample plus clearly divergent signature cards. Camps are a staged overlay — applied for analysis, never silently promoted to taxonomy — and decks that fit no camp stay honestly [unlabeled]. Camp field share = parent share × the camp's fraction of the parent's current-window decks.
  • No significance theater — the clustering gate is stability-based on purpose: running a t-test on data the clustering already saw would double-dip and inflate false positives. Where a real multiple-testing control exists (era boundaries), it's BH-FDR; where it can't exist honestly, the page shows sample sizes and tiers instead.
