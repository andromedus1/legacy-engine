    What is Agency %? — the metric and each column
    Agency % = min( adjusted field win rate , worst measured matchup ) × 100 — how much of the match result your own play keeps in the worst case. The adjusted field WR is your available shrunk matchup estimates weighted by the current field's archetype shares and renormalized over that available share (are you competitive in general?). Thin estimates may inform this average, but they cannot set the floor. The worst measured matchup is the minimum shrunk estimate among cells that clear the table's selected matchup-n gate (are you competitive against anyone?). A high-agency deck never hands the result to the pairing screen: it concedes no free wins to the room. Theory under test: maximum agency = most fun.
    • Measured Agency cells only — a matchup counts when it clears the table's selected minimum matchup n (the generated default is --ground-n, normally 8). Era-windowed cells are preferred; when the era window is below that gate, a fallback cell is used — and the fallback pools matches only since the last ban that affected either deck in the pair (ran a banned card in ≥25% of its pre-ban lists), labeled BA date. A true full-corpus cell (FC) exists only when neither deck was ever ban-affected. The Nadu rule: a banned engine's matches never inflate a row — in either direction. This is the mature Agency/P(best) ledger; the separate direct estimate can recover older clean intervals without changing this gate.
    • Every measured matchup can set the floor — once a cell clears the selected measured-evidence gate, its shrunk estimate participates in the minimum. This makes visible holes matter sooner. Because a deck with more measured opponents has more chances to expose a low matchup, incomplete rows remain explicit upper bounds and coverage must be read alongside agency.
    • Coverage counts against the floor — unmeasured field share is shown per row; a floor claim over 70% of the field is a 70% claim.
    • Blowouts — count of measured current-field matchups where you're getting run over: raw WR < 40% is a full blowout, 40–45% is a half. The chip shows full✕ + half½ of N measured. Floor is your single worst hole; blowout count is how many holes — a richer read on "competitive against anyone." Classified on the observed record; cells below the measured evidence gate are excluded entirely, so a shrinkage prior is never called a blowout.
    • % meta that blows you out — the blowout count weighted by field share: the full share of every sub-40% opponent plus half the share of every 40–45% opponent. This is how much of the room you sit down against at a structural disadvantage — a small count of blowouts against big-share decks matters more than many against fringe brews. Measured share only, so it's a lower bound (unmeasured opponents can't be counted).
    • Positive matchup edges — the inverse raw-WR bands appear in each expanded ledger: edge at 55–60% and dominant above 60%. They use the same measured-cell gate as blowouts and are descriptive highlights only; they do not change any row metric or ranking.
    • Grounded rows — the generated method requires the top --top-k field opponents (normally 8) each to clear the selected matchup-n gate and at least --cover-min of field share-mass (normally 80%) to be measured. Rows failing either test are listed separately in gray: their agency number is an upper-bound lean, not a floor claim.
    • Posterior lean is diagnostic — the opt-in view removes the hard sample cliff: every field opponent contributes to a seeded smooth-floor posterior in proportion to field share and evidence precision. Era evidence is preferred without an n threshold, fallback is used only when era evidence is absent, and unresolved opponents retain a weak explicit prior. The displayed lean is Q25 with median and 95% interval in the row detail. It does not change P(best), evidence strata, or the gated recommendation.
    • Direct matchup estimate is diagnostic — this column is a field-weighted average of direct matchup cells from the exact union of eligible clean intervals. For a deck touched by a short-lived banned card, that can join clean pre-exposure history to post-ban results while excluding the card's entire exposure gap; unaffected pairs retain their eligible history. The row shows direct n, covered field share, and how many clean-history matches were added. It improves the readable estimate but does not change Agency, its floor, grounding, P(best), or ordering.
    How the numbers are made — matches, shrinkage, era windows, camps
    • Where matches come from — decklists are mirrored from the public tournament cache (MTGO + Melee + CardsRealm) and labeled with archetypes by a rules engine. Matchup win rates come only from rounds-bearing events (Challenges and paper events publish pairings; MTGO leagues publish decklists but no rounds). So a deck's meta share and its matchup sample are different-sized populations — a big field share with thin matchup cells is normal, not a bug. A match counts when one player took strictly more games ("2-1", "2-0"); draws and byes are excluded from win rates.
    • What "current field" means — the observed current field is every published decklist since the latest confirmed B&R change (the audit header names the ban). If that sample is below 500 decks, ranking uses a transition-stabilized decision field: it adds a bounded count-weighted prior from the immediately preceding regime; affected archetypes are excluded from that preceding-regime prior. The audit reports both observed and effective counts; % of meta and matchup weighting use the decision field. Within each slice, raw counts apply — one published list = one vote, no win-rate weighting. A field reset does not erase matchup history: matchup eligibility is resolved independently by pair. Know what gets published, because the field inherits that bias: MTGO leagues publish only 5-0 lists, challenges publish top finishers, paper events publish whatever the venue uploads — and online + paper are blended here (the engine tracks venue splits, but this page doesn't). Decklists the labeling rules can't classify surface as Unknown rather than being dropped. Note the two "currents": the field window is the ban regime; the ◦not current marker uses a shorter horizon (the last 4 corpus weeks), because "legal in this regime" and "actually being played this month" are different questions.
    • Why two win rates per matchup — raw is literally wins/n: what happened, but noisy at small n (a 3-1 cell reads 75% and means almost nothing). The headline number is a Beta-Binomial shrunk estimate: it behaves as if the cell started with ~15 phantom matches at a prior win rate, so small samples get pulled toward the prior and big samples converge to raw. The gap between the two numbers is itself a signal — a big gap means "small sample, don't trust raw".
    • The prior is hierarchical, not flat — a matchup cell shrinks toward the deck's own overall win rate (itself first shrunk toward 50%), so an unknown matchup for a strong deck reads as "probably decent", not "coin flip". A camp's cell shrinks toward its parent archetype's same matchup with that camp's decks removed — camps inherit the parent's profile until their own data speaks. When an era boundary truncates a cell below n=100, the pre-boundary value serves as its prior (labeled in the cell's provenance), so history informs but never impersonates the current era.
    • Era windows ("since" column) — every deck's history is bucketed by week and scanned by four change detectors: card-presence cliffs/ramps (a ban or a new-set adoption is a visible step), composition shifts in the flex slots, share-of-field breaks, and win-rate corroboration (never primary evidence on its own). Nearby candidates merge; the whole fleet of boundaries then passes one Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery screen (α=0.05) so ~150 entities screened every refresh don't accumulate false eras; a surviving boundary is still rejected if too few decks exist after it. The last accepted boundary is the deck's stable_since; each boundary is attributed to a ban/release when the evidence names one (e.g. "release: Flow State adoption"), and an unattributed disturbance raises a drift alarm instead of silently re-windowing.
    • How an Agency/P(best) cell picks its window — a matchup A-vs-B pools matches back to the later of the two decks' stable_since dates (both sides must be in their current form). If that era window falls below the selected matchup-n gate, a fallback cell is shown instead — pooled since the later of the two decks' ban-affectedness dates (labeled BA date), or full history (FC) only when neither deck was ever materially hit by a ban. More data, may blend older builds of the same legal engine — but never a banned one: the Nadu-era Cephalid and Candelabra-era Forge matches are structurally excluded from every fallback that touches those decks.
    • How the direct estimate recovers clean history — each archetype receives explicit eligible intervals. A localized card exposure creates pre-exposure, excluded-gap, and post-ban atoms; pair eligibility is their exact intersection, so a physical match is selected once only when both decks were clean. The report prefers that expanded direct view when it contributes observed history, otherwise it uses current direct results. Provenance labels are based on matches actually admitted, not merely on eligibility. This diagnostic view is kept separate from the mature Agency ledger and proof gate above.
    • The matchup-n control recomputes the page — changing it reselects each archetype/camp cell from its embedded era and ban-scoped fallback candidates, then recomputes adjusted field WR, floor, agency, blowouts, coverage, grounding, labels, and sorting in the browser. Strategic-plan cells use the same selected gate over their uniform field window. Cross-camp P(best) is a generated Monte-Carlo result, so it becomes n/a whenever the selected gate differs from the generated --ground-n rather than showing a stale score.
    • Sample tiers — tier words on this page (e.g. the field-basis tier in the audit header) are fixed thresholds on n: speculative <30, evolving 30–99, established ≥100.
    • Camps (subarchetypes) — found by clustering each archetype's mainboard flex slots (TF-IDF → SVD → HDBSCAN), then gated: the split must survive bootstrap re-clustering (co-membership stability ≥0.9 over 50 resamples) and every camp needs a real sample plus clearly divergent signature cards. Camps are a staged overlay — applied for analysis, never silently promoted to taxonomy — and decks that fit no camp stay honestly [unlabeled]. Camp field share = parent share × the camp's fraction of the parent's current-window decks. All camp matchup cells come from ONE multi-split matrix built over every staged parent at once — numerically identical to the old one-matrix-per-parent builds (parity-tested in the engine), but every camp now coexists in a single matrix.
    • Cross-camp P(best) — the camp table's P(best) column comes from one shared-field Monte-Carlo: sample the field once per draw (Dirichlet over the parent-level window counts, fixed seed), score every candidate — all camps plus all unsplit archetypes — against that same sampled field, and count how often each is the argmax. Sharing the field draw is what makes the number comparable across camps of different parents. Cells are the same era-preferred / ban-scoped ones the ledger shows; candidates below 5% measured coverage are excluded (their score would be imputation noise) and show n/a instead.
    • Strategic plans are direct match aggregates — every current archetype has one curated primary plan; optional secondary labels explain hybrids without duplicating field share or matches. Plan-vs-plan cells pool decisive matches directly rather than averaging rendered archetype rates. A plan's own diagonal is structural 50% context: exact mirrors and cross-archetype same-plan matches are reported separately, but the diagonal is never measured, never sets the floor, and is excluded from external coverage. Every archetype dropdown's five plan cells are likewise built directly from that archetype's decisive non-mirror matches. Composition-derived superarchetypes may support internal matrix borrowing, but they contribute no page-visible lean and no ranking input.
    • No significance theater — the clustering gate is stability-based on purpose: running a t-test on data the clustering already saw would double-dip and inflate false positives. Where a real multiple-testing control exists (era boundaries), it's BH-FDR; where it can't exist honestly, the page shows sample sizes and tiers instead.

    Agency map — general strength vs floor

    One dot per archetype with a computable adjusted WR and floor. Blue = grounded floor: the configured top field opponents are each measured at the generated gate and the configured field-share coverage floor is met, so the floor is a measured claim. Gray = incomplete floor (upper bound): that grounding failed — unknown matchups sit near the deck's own shrunk average, and agency = min over cells can only fall as more get measured — so the dot's floor is an upper bound, not a claim. Filled dot = current; hollow dot = not current (fewer than 5 decks in the last 4 corpus weeks). Labels mark the top 5 grounded-and-current decks by agency; hover any dot for its numbers.

    Archetypes

    %
    Click a row to expand its full matchup ledger — one line per current-field opponent: the chip after the name is that opponent's % of meta; the first number is the shrunk estimate, raw is wins/n; the window chip is era (era-windowed), BA date (fallback since the later ban that affected either deck), or FC (full corpus — neither deck ever ban-affected); "unmeasured" = below the table's selected minimum matchup n, excluded from floor and blowouts.
    Column notes: decks/4wk = the deck's corpus count over the last 4 corpus weeks; ◦not current marks names with fewer than 5 decks in that span (historical form, not a live pick); window = the row's era window (since date = stable_since from era detection; full history = no accepted era boundary); % of meta = transition-stabilized decision-field share (the audit header separates observed and effective counts); grounded row = the configured top field opponents each clear the selected matchup-n gate and the generated minimum field-share coverage is met — its floor is broadly measured; ≤ plus the upper bound marker means grounding failed and the agency value can fall as more matchups become measured. Rank stability is the row's complete rank range across raw, CI-gated, ban-scoped, and era-only projections; n/a names any projection that could not rank it. Expanded ungrounded rows turn the same top-k/coverage rule into a match-collection agenda.

    Subarchetypes (camps)

    %
    Same columns, ledger, and markers as the archetype table above, plus P(best): the probability this deck is the best call for the current field — one shared Monte-Carlo where every camp and every unsplit archetype is scored against the same sampled field (parent-level Dirichlet, fixed seed; parameters in the audit header), on the same era-preferred / ban-scoped cells the ledger shows. Because all candidates share one field draw, the values are comparable across camps of different parents — the single number the old per-parent matrices could never provide. Candidates need ≥ 5% measured field coverage; rows below that show n/a with their coverage (their score would be pure imputation). The chip is the risk-adjusted lower-quartile field WR (Q25); S* marks coverage < 85% — a full-field estimate leaning on imputation for the unmeasured share, including each camp's own parent (that cell is absent by construction).
    Camp % of meta = parent share × camp fraction among the parent's field-window decks. The evidence column reports measured/imputed field share and the typed stratum. Historical-only camps stay visible as inactive; unsupported rows show P(best)=n/a. The grouping control reorganizes these same rows under evidence headings without changing scores or hiding inactive evidence. The posterior-lean control may re-sort within these existing honesty strata, but never changes candidacy, P(best), or which rows are called grounded. [unlabeled] = the parent's decks that fit no discovered camp (cluster noise / new builds the frozen membership hasn't seen) — surfaced honestly, never absorbed into a camp. Camp labels are staged-candidate provenance (speculative overlay, not promoted taxonomy).
