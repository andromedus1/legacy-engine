      Method & sources

      Current data.

      Sources & selection. Published Legacy decklists and tournament rounds come from the fbettega tournament cache, with card metadata from Scryfall. Decklists are classified using the current archetype rules and camp assignments. Lists describe what was published, not a census of all entrants: leagues and events without round records can inform field shares but cannot supply head-to-head results. Matchup records use resolvable, decisive rounds; draws, byes, forfeits and ambiguous or unmatched players are excluded. A list count and a matchup n therefore measure different populations.

      The current field. The displayed field starts at the stated field boundary and ends at the latest included event date. Recent lists receive exponentially more weight, using a 28-day half-life: a list 28 days older carries half the weight. Exact sightings and the effective sample size after weighting remain separate. When the current field is very small, bounded historical field support can supplement it; that support is identified as a prior, not new observations. Source coverage is not assumed complete, and an online-heavy sample need not describe your local room.

      Using history. Field composition and matchup evidence have different windows. An older match can still inform a current pairing when it falls in compatible periods for both decks. Pair-specific selection respects their applicable era and ban boundaries; localized checks can recover older periods where the relevant disruption was absent. The selected direct interval evidence takes precedence; otherwise the era estimate, then its ban-scoped fallback, supplies the cell. These are alternative views of evidence, not samples added together. Each dropdown records the chosen source, window and prior.

      Estimating a matchup. Each cell combines selected wins and losses with a fitted Beta prior: (wins + prior mean × prior strength) / (matches + prior strength). Priors borrow strength from relevant historical or broader deck evidence, so a few matches do not produce extreme certainty. More direct results progressively outweigh the prior. A supplied cell with no direct results retains its fitted prior; a completely absent cell gets a weak Beta(1, 1) prior centered on 50%. Thin and unobserved pairings stay visible. The coverage filter does not decide which observations enter the estimate.

      Performance & matchup floor. Performance is the full-field weighted average of matchup estimates, including mirrors at 50%. Matchup floor is the lowest estimate against any positive-share non-mirror opponent, however rare. Changing share magnitudes affects performance; changing which opponents are present can change the floor. The model's floor recommendation maximizes floor independently, with performance breaking ties. Column sorting lets you compare either measure. Blue tradeoffs are decks for which no shown eligible peer is better on both measures.

      Reading the ranges. Seeded posterior draws produce 95% conditional posterior intervals for performance and the minimum across opponents. Performance intervals also sample field shares when count evidence is supplied; share-only scenarios keep those weights fixed. The range beside the floor is the named toughest pairing's interval; the all-opponent minimum interval is under Evidence details. Floor coverage is the non-mirror field share meeting your chosen minimum matchup n. Filters change the rows shown, not their estimates. These intervals condition on fitted priors and do not capture every pilot, event or publication-selection effect. A high estimated floor is useful evidence, not a guarantee of no bad matchups.

      Plans, archetypes & builds. Strategic plans summarize broad game plans; their direct-match aggregates are coarser than deck rankings. Camp rows expose different builds within an archetype. Build differences compares camp floors on the same full external opponent field and separates pooling uplift from the parent's actual floor gap. Composition and pilot overlap help interpret that gap; they do not establish a causal advantage. Current globally supported candidates define eligibility; prior-only estimates remain labeled, and inactive decks can be shown with the filter.

      Local fields & refresh changes. A supplied field reweights the same matchup cells while keeping global candidate eligibility. Counts, declared effective sample size and fixed shares remain distinct; unknown opponents keep their positive mass with weak priors. Local plan shares are available, but plan performance and floors are omitted until their internal deck mixture can be reweighted coherently. What changed compares compatible published snapshots, separating performance movement into field-weight and matchup-estimate contributions. This is arithmetic attribution, not a causal claim.

      Checking the method. The historical check below evaluates the served parent-archetype model at six cutoffs. Predictions were sealed before later outcomes were opened; development selected a challenger before the separate confirmation period. Lower log loss and Brier scores mean better probabilistic forecasts. Current, half-strength, doubled-strength and opponent-plan priors were compared. Doubling helped development but did not retain its advantage on confirmation, so the current prior remains. Fixed retrospective taxonomy and sparse later floor outcomes limit what this study establishes; it does not certify a local field or suppress current estimates.
